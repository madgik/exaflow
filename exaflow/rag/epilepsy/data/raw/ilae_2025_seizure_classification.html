Updated Classification of Epileptic Seizures (2025)

Source snapshot for the epilepsy RAG MVP based on the ILAE guideline page and intended for local retrieval prototyping.

Overview

The International League Against Epilepsy updated the operational classification of epileptic seizures in 2025, building on the 2017 framework and incorporating implementation experience and expert review.

Main Seizure Classes

The updated classification keeps four main seizure classes: focal, generalized, unknown whether focal or generalized, and unclassified.

Key Changes

The revised framework removes the word onset from the names of the four main classes. It distinguishes classifiers from descriptors and uses consciousness rather than awareness as a classifier. The structure is intended to support clinically meaningful classification while reducing unnecessary complexity.

Focal Seizures

Focal seizures arise in networks limited to one hemisphere at onset. Their expanded classification can include descriptors and features that clarify the seizure presentation.

Consciousness and Responsiveness

In the 2025 update, consciousness is defined operationally by both awareness and responsiveness. This change affects how focal seizure subtypes are described in clinical communication.

Older and Updated Terminology

Older language such as simple partial seizure and complex partial seizure should be interpreted against the updated focal seizure terminology used in contemporary ILAE guidance.

Generalized Seizures

Generalized seizures engage bilaterally distributed networks from onset. Their classification remains distinct from focal seizures because onset category directly affects clinical reasoning and management.

Scope Notes

Neonatal seizures are not included in this updated seizure classification. They are addressed in a separate ILAE position paper.

Governance

This classification is positioned as an authoritative ILAE reference for terminology and seizure type classification in epilepsy-focused systems.
